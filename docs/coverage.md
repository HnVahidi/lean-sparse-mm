# Coverage of the manuscript

This map follows the eleven numbered claims in the ten-page manuscript included in the companion. It compares the manuscript statements to the Lean declarations and their assumptions. The [manuscript record](manuscript.md) identifies the exact source snapshot and PDF.

**Exact** means that Lean states the indicated mathematical property in the finite model. **Equivalent conclusion** means that the checked result gives the manuscript's guarantee through a different construction, an integral parameter choice, or explicit constants. **Restricted** identifies a part of a claim that the checked interface supports. **Missing** identifies a statement or implementation detail outside the checked results.

## Model and parameter correspondence

- `Mask n` is a finite set of coordinates of an `n × n` matrix. `UniformSparse` bounds every row and column. `SupportedBy` requires zero outside an input mask and permits zero on marked coordinates. `Q` requests only selected entries. The full product may be dense. [Masks and input support](../SparseMM/Masks.lean)
- The input masks, output mask, and combined left/right input distribution are public. The hypothesis limits the initial input load to `K*d` per processor. The round bound is uniform in `n,d` for fixed `K`. The theorem takes `n,d > 0` and covers the paper's `d ≤ n` range. Each private numerical input occurs only in its original holder's view. [Main theorem and local views](../SparseMM/Main.lean)
- Arithmetic is over Mathlib's `Semiring`. Each term has the order `U i j * V j k`, so multiplicative commutativity is unnecessary. Final answers, including zero, are proved at the assigned holders. [Arithmetic and computation](../SparseMM/Algebra.lean)
- The operational model allows at most one send and one receive in each synchronous round. Local steps read their own state and actual received messages. The public plan fixes finite choices before the values. The existence theorem provides a finite program for each public instance. A uniform executable planner and a local computation time bound are outside the checked claims. [Program and execution model](../SparseMM/LocalPipeline.lean)
- The formal round bound counts scalar words. `Wire.encodedReceive_eq_model` proves per-round equivalence when a decoder inverts the encoder on actually transmitted values. An end-to-end theorem over a fixed finite alphabet of `O(log n)` bits is missing. The paper assumes a lossless encoding of the used inputs, products, and partial sums. Its bit-model interpretation uses that premise. [Wire encoding](../SparseMM/Wire.lean)
- The exact integral construction uses `h = Nat.sqrt d + 1` and `t = d*h`. Lean proves `t ≤ 2d√d` for `d > 0`. The output-count bound `≤ d` accounts for the ceiling implicit in cyclic counts. The theorem for the alternative assignment gives the same strict residual load bound. [Integral rounding](../SparseMM/Rounding.lean)

## Verification scope

[Challenge](../Challenge.lean) states the advertised claims independently of [Solution](../Solution.lean). The per-claim column distinguishes [direct Comparator entries](../comparator.json), proofs used by selected declarations, and compared definitions or constructor signatures. The dependency paths follow theorem calls in the proof sources. A compiled supporting theorem has its own direct statement comparison only when listed in the configuration.

The shared logarithmic cost conversions have direct Comparator entries for `ToolboxComplexity.log_div_le_rounds`, `ToolboxComplexity.clog_two_le_log_div_add_one`, `ToolboxComplexity.pipeline_rounds_le`, and `ToolboxComplexity.single_value_rounds_le_log`.

The introduction's comparisons with earlier algorithms, the conditional lower-bound curves, and the conclusion's triangle-coverage remark are outside the checked results. The numbered claims concern the supported model with public input distribution. Open questions about removing support or improving the exponent remain open in this companion.
